PART / 01

Corner starter plate

Flat guide plate carrying the 2×2 seed: four metal-bush seats, two clamp holes, and pockets that receive the fence tabs.

PART / 02

Corner fence

Separate L wall that registers against both board edges. Its tabs drop into the plate pockets; glue square after a dry fit.

PART / 03

Indexing tile

Ribbed body with four pin sockets and a field of drill guides. Prints flat so the tile stays rigid at 170 mm.

PART / 04

Locating pin set

Four removable pins printed in one plate. Press into the tile sockets, chamfered end down, to carry the seed forward.

CASE STUDY / TWO-TOOL SEED + TILE

From one true corner to a repeatable grid.

The design separates two jobs: establish a trustworthy first pattern, then use that finished pattern as the reference for every later move.

01 / REGISTER

Start from the panel corner

The corner starter's L fence bears on both outside edges. Four metal-bushed guides put down a 2×2 seed at the selected edge offset and pitch. Clamp the plate before drilling.

02 / MATCH

Carry the same 2×2 pattern

The indexing tile has four pins in the same square spacing as the starter's four seed holes. Seating all four pins constrains the tile in both directions and reduces rocking during the next pass.

03 / BATCH

Drill the remaining stations

Every other station on the tile is a guide. The default 4×4 tile makes twelve new holes in one setup. Lift the tool between passes, then seat the pins in four finished holes.

04 / CUSTOMIZE

Let the board pattern drive the model

Set rows, columns, hole diameter, spacing, edge offset, and panel thickness. The tool preview updates with those values. The A1 mini bed sets the upper bound for tile rows and columns; larger pitch or wider bush bosses reduce the available guide count.

05 / CUTTER PROFILE

Start with a twist drill

The first profile pairs a normal twist drill with a hardened metal guide bush whose inside diameter matches the bit. A wider cutter profile comes later with a pilot guide and a separate clearance opening.

BUILD NOTES / DESIGN BOUNDARIES

What the first v3 case study assumes

Starting pattern

The opening values are 10 mm holes, 50 mm pitch, 25 mm edge offset, and 18 mm board thickness. The 25 mm edge offset was an assumption in the previous design; confirm it against the panel before drilling. Change all values in the configurator to compare alternate patterns.

Printed and purchased parts

  • Corner starter plate and separate L fence, glued square after a dry fit.
  • Indexing tile body and four removable printed locating pins.
  • Metal guide bushes matched to the selected twist bit, plus clamps and a sacrificial backer.

Fit and print limits

The A1 mini build area is 180 × 180 mm. At the default pitch and bush size, a 4×4 tile measures 170 × 170 mm and leaves 5 mm to each bed edge. The configurator reports the largest square layout that fits and rejects station spacing that would collide with the guide bosses.

Accuracy still to establish

Printed bush seats, removable pin sockets, fence tabs, and repeated indexing need physical fit checks. The model communicates nominal geometry and workflow; a fit coupon and scrap-board run establish the final clearances for the actual printer, filament, bush, bit, and panel.

This release models the regular twist-drill setup. Forstner bits and hole saws have cutting heads much wider than their pilots, so their next configuration needs its own guide-and-clearance geometry rather than a simple larger hole value.
